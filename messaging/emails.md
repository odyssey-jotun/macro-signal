**Subject:** The one report behind every inflation headline

Every headline about inflation traces back to one report: the Consumer Price Index.

It matters for your money because when prices rise quickly, the Federal Reserve can keep interest rates higher for longer. That reaches mortgages, car loans and savings accounts.

### Nurture 2: payroll jobs

**Subject:** Why the jobs number changes after it comes out

Hey,

The jobs report comes out the first Friday of the month at 8:30 a.m. Eastern. The headline number is payroll jobs: how many jobs employers added or cut last month, outside of farm work.

Here's the part many people miss. The Bureau of Labor Statistics revises the two previous months in every new report, as more employers send in their numbers. A month that looked strong can look weaker a few weeks later.

So when you see a big jobs headline, check the revisions too.

Macro Signal reads the revisions along with the new number and emails you both.

### Nurture 3: the unemployment rate

**Subject:** Who counts as unemployed

Hey,

Someone counts as unemployed if they want a job, are looking for one and don't have one. A person who stopped looking isn't counted.

The unemployment rate comes out in the same report as payroll jobs, usually the first Friday of the month. The Federal Reserve's job is to keep prices stable and employment high, so it watches this number closely.

**Subject:** The decision behind your mortgage rate

Hey,

Eight times a year, the Federal Reserve meets and announces a decision at 2:00 p.m. Eastern. It sets a target range for the federal funds rate, the rate banks charge each other to borrow overnight.

That one rate ripples out to the rates on mortgages, car loans, credit cards and savings accounts. When it moves, borrowing and saving change for everyone.
