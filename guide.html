The free economic report guide

The four reports my AI reads first, on one page: what each one measures, who publishes it and when it comes out.

Download the PDF

Consumer Price Index

CUSR0000SA0

How much the prices people pay for everyday goods and services changed, from groceries and gas to rent.

Published by
U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics
Comes out
Monthly, around the middle of the month, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern
Why investors watch it
It is the best-known measure of inflation. When prices rise quickly, the Federal Reserve can keep interest rates higher for longer.

Payroll jobs

CES0000000001

How many jobs employers added or cut last month, outside of farm work.

Published by
U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, in the monthly jobs report
Comes out
Usually the first Friday of the month, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern
Why investors watch it
It is one of the clearest signs of whether the economy is growing or slowing, and it often moves markets the morning it comes out.

Unemployment rate

LNS14000000

The share of people who want a job, are looking for one and don't have one.

Published by
U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, in the same jobs report
Comes out
Usually the first Friday of the month, at 8:30 a.m. Eastern
Why investors watch it
The Federal Reserve's job is to keep prices stable and employment high, so it watches this number closely.

Federal funds rate

RIFSPFF_N.M

The interest rate banks charge each other to borrow overnight. The Federal Reserve sets a target range for it.

Published by
Federal Reserve
Comes out
Decided at eight scheduled meetings a year, announced at 2:00 p.m. Eastern
Why investors watch it
It ripples out to the rates on mortgages, car loans, credit cards and savings accounts.
